What you see

Type in any chapter and the page changes. Nothing needs saving. The pages follow your cursor: click somewhere in your text, or move with the arrow keys, and the preview scrolls to that place, with a brief ripple to show you where. While you’re typing, it follows quietly, without the ripple, so the page doesn’t flash at you.

Click a word on a page and its source opens, selected, with the line level with where you clicked, so your eye doesn’t have to move to find it. Select text on a page and the same words are selected in your text, ready to comment on or send to the assistant.

Typst comes inside Foreword, so the preview works with nothing else installed.

Mistakes, without a blank page

A mistake in the markup gets a red underline where it is, with Typst’s message when you rest the pointer on it, and the status bar counts it (“1 error”; click to go to it). Meanwhile the pages keep showing the last version that worked, so you can still see the chapter while you fix it. Typst’s messages are terse. Ask the assistant to fix it in the status bar, or Fix This Error with the Assistant on the right-click menu, explains the mistake in plain words and fixes it.

Just this chapter, for a long book

Every change sets the whole book again. For a few hundred pages that’s quick. For a very long book, say 700 pages, the preview starts to trail behind your typing. So it can show just the chapter you’re in: choose Chapter above the preview, or in Settings ▸ General ▸ Preview ▸ Shows, and it keeps up as you type. Open another chapter and the preview moves to it.

The chapter is set with your book’s own design, so apart from the page numbers it looks as it will in the book. Nothing is copied or changed in your files to do it.

On a long book the preview also lets go of pages far from where you are, and draws them again as you scroll back, so it doesn’t run out of memory after you’ve read through the whole thing. If it ever has to reload, it comes back to where you were.

Export

Export PDF… (⇧⌘E) asks where to save, starting where you saved this book’s PDF last time, and says where it went. If the book has code examples that are out of date, it offers to run them first, so a PDF never goes out with old output. Open PDF (⌥⌘E) opens the last one. The Cover view above the preview shows the print cover, with its guides.

What it doesn’t do

  • With one chapter shown, page numbers and references to other chapters are only right in the whole book.
  • Clicking a page finds the words behind it, but not every line of code or maths can be found that way.
  • The preview hides while a dialog is open.
  • Error messages are Typst’s own. The plain-words version comes from the assistant, when you ask.
