Books

Novel
Fiction in chapters, with scene breaks, a title page and running headers.
Non-fiction
Ideas and advice: contents, an introduction, sections, footnotes, sources and boxed asides.
Textbook
Teaching material: numbered sections, equations, figures, definitions, examples and exercises.
Programming book
A technical book whose code examples run, show their real output, and can check it.
Picture book
A short illustrated story: pictures across the page or a spread, a line or two of words on each, and sound words drawn in.
Blank
A title page and one chapter. Everything else is up to you.

Each one is a complete, ordinary Typst book: a file for the book, one for its design, and one for each chapter, with notes saying what each part does. Nothing in it depends on Foreword, so anyone with Typst can build it. Margins scale with the page, so a small paperback isn’t left with a sliver of text in the middle.

Each template offers the sizes that suit it, and you can type in any other.

Papers

IEEE, ACM, NeurIPS, CVPR, AAAI, ACL, Springer LNCS, arXiv, AMS and APA 7, each from its template on Typst Universe, Typst’s library of templates, downloaded when you create the paper. A note says which free fonts it wants and how to install them, and the message box is filled in (not sent) with a request to the assistant to set your title and authors.

Picture books

A picture book is pages of pictures with a few words on each, and the template keeps a page that simple to write. This is the page in the picture at the top:

#story-page(
  picture: "illustrations/07-chin.jpg",
  at: top,
  sounds: {
    sound("rub", x: 17.1%, y: 19.6%, angle: -14deg, size: 23pt)
    sound("rub", x: 22.7%, y: 25.8%, angle: -8deg, size: 26pt)
    sound("rub!", x: 28.4%, y: 32.5%, angle: -3deg, size: 30pt)
  },
)[
  Sometimes Luca rubs his chin on you.
]

Words stay clear of the edges and the binding, and pictures run past the edge so the printer can trim them (see printing). A spread across two pages has to start on a left-hand page. If one wouldn’t, the book tells you to add or remove a page before it, rather than letting it print split across a sheet.

Adding a chapter

File ▸ New Chapter… (⌥⌘N) asks for a title. It names the new file the way your other chapters are named, and adds the chapter to the book after the last one, before any back matter.

What it doesn’t do

  • Paper templates need the internet the first time, to download them.
  • Sizes outside the US and UK are the commonly quoted ones. Check your printer’s exact figure.
